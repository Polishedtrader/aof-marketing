## The mission (Paul, 25 Nov 2026)
- Most kitchen companies still run their INTERNAL processes on paper: job folders, job sheets, diaries,
  whiteboards, order and cutting lists, install schedules, phone calls and texts between office, factory and installers.
  The only digital parts are islands - e-signing (maybe), design software, factory machines (CNC) - and they
  don't talk to each other.
- AOF is ONE system that talks from enquiry to marketing: enquiry -> quote -> signing -> design -> factory ->
  install -> customer care -> reviews/marketing, all connected, in the business's own name.
- Positioning (Paul, 25 Nov 2026): we are the company that FIXES ISSUES - not a software seller.
  Lead with the owner's problems (missed enquiries, chasing, wrong orders, double handling, "where are we up to?"
  calls, late nights on paperwork), then show the fix. On visits, open with "What's costing you the most time?"
- Proof it works (Paul, internal - never name the customer publicly): what won the accepted $17,990 sale was
  exactly this - fixing their issues, and the branded client app that makes the business look good to its
  clients while clients always know where their job is at - so they STAND OUT FROM THEIR COMPETITORS (other
  kitchen companies still sending texts and paper). Lead with these.
- The "wow" moment (Paul): in the first meeting the owner saw THEIR logo on a phone as an app. The first email
  campaign is built around recreating that moment - hence the logo rule (no logo = no email). On visits, show the
  owner their own app on Paul's phone (officeflow /preview/apps with their logo) to recreate it in person.
- Core belief (Paul): "Time is the one thing we can't get more of." Lead with time.
- What the owner gets (Paul, 25 Nov 2026):
  - Saves time / time management: the owner and team know what's on, what's next and who's where, without
    chasing - the system plans and reminds so nobody's day is lost to phone calls and paperwork.
  - A system that thinks for you: it knows the next step and does it or prompts it.
  - It remembers what's been sent and when (quotes, plans, orders, invoices, reminders) - nothing relies on memory.
  - Everyone kept in the loop through the apps: customers and installers see where the job is, under the
    business's own brand, so the "where are we up to?" calls stop.
  - The apps send reminders to the team and to clients: what's needed next (selections, sign-offs, payments,
    site ready) and when trades are arriving (measure, delivery, install, plumber, electrician, stone).
  - Reviews come in automatically at the end of each job, feeding the marketing.
- AOF is here to upset that space and move them all digital.
- So the real competitor is paper and "the way we've always done it", not other software.
  Main audience: owners on paper who've never bought office software (not people switching from Jobman).
  Message: going digital without the pain - built around how you already work, set up for you, staff and AI
  training included, and your customers see your brand.
