### 2b. Targeted paid ads to kitchen/joinery OWNERS — PROPOSED (Paul asked 25 Nov; meeting 27 Nov)
- What: a 4-week, capped test from 12 Jan 2027 (not Dec: shops are flat out and then shut).
  a) LinkedIn: upload our own list of businesses (queue.csv company names + websites) as a company list and target
     Owner / Director / Founder / Managing Director titles at those companies. Ad: a 30-45 s video of Paul
     ("25 years running a kitchen business, so I built the office I wanted") → book a 15-min look.
     About $25/day.
  b) Meta (Facebook/Instagram): retarget people who visited the demo page, plus custom audiences from our list.
     Needs the Meta pixel on the demo site (the code chat does that). About $10/day.
  c) Google Search: exact phrases like "kitchen business software", "joinery job management software",
     "cabinet maker software". Small volume, but every click is someone looking. About $10/day.
- Why: the market is small and countable (we already have 326 named businesses), so the money should go only on
  those owners, not on broad audiences. Evidence and limits:
  - LinkedIn B2B clicks are dear, about $5-12 each, and retargeting about $3-6
    (https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/linkedin-average-cost-per-click; agency figures, likely USD).
  - Meta detailed targeting is now mostly a "suggestion", so Meta can't reliably pick out business owners. It is
    better at retargeting and custom audiences (https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-ads-targeting-2026/). More targeting
    options were removed from 15 Jan 2026
    (https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-removes-more-detailed-ad-targeting-options-facebook-instagram/757856/).
  - Print: The Australian Woodworker is from ~$930 a quarter page (2023 rates,
    https://www.skillspublish.com.au/ad-rates-specs/), but it is hobbyist-heavy, so it's a poor fit. ACFA's Connect
    Magazine (free, cabinetmaking/joinery) doesn't publish rates; ask at advertising@connectmagazine.com.au.
- Risk: LinkedIn needs a minimum audience size, and small shops' owners are often not on LinkedIn. If our list
  is too small, widen to Owner/Director titles in furniture/cabinetry and construction industries, NSW only.
- Cost: capped at ~$1,200 for 4 weeks (about $45/day) + 1 hr of Paul's time (record the video, approve ads).
  The team sets up drafts; Paul pays and presses go.
- Expected: honest guess, 100-200 clicks → 2-5 owner conversations. One sale pays for years of this.
- Test and kill rule: if $600 is spent with zero owner conversations or demo bookings, stop. Keep a channel if it
  costs under ~$300 per owner conversation.
- Status: proposed. Ranked below the warm intros (1, 2) because money buys attention, not trust.

### 1. Proof from the two existing customers + owner-to-owner referrals — PROPOSED (meeting 27 Nov)
- What: Paul (himself, not the team) asks each of his two customers for (a) OK to name them and a 60-90 s phone video
  or 3-line quote on what changed, and (b) the names of 2-3 owners they know and respect, plus an intro text.
- Why: Paul has two paying customers and zero proof on the website or in emails. Peer word of mouth is how trade
  owners buy; referred B2B leads convert far better than cold (industry surveys put referral close rates several
  times cold; e.g. https://referralrock.com/blog/b2b-referral-marketing-statistics/ — vendor data, treat as direction
  not fact). ServiceM8 grew with a partner program of locals helping new users (https://servicem8.com/about-us).
  Proof also lifts every other channel (email, visits, social, supplier reps).
- Cost: ~30-45 min of Paul's time, $0 (optionally a thank-you: one month's support free per referral that buys).
- Expected: 2-6 warm owner names; 1 usable quote/video. If 4 warm intros → ~1 sale in 90 days.
- Test: ask both by 4 Dec. Keep if ≥2 warm intros or 1 usable proof piece by 11 Dec.
- Note: campaign rule says the team never contacts these two businesses. This is Paul asking personally; Paul decides.

### 2. Supplier reps as door-openers (board, hardware, benchtop) — PROPOSED (meeting 27 Nov)
- What: Paul has a coffee with 2 local sales reps / branch managers on the Mid North Coast (board supplier, hardware,
  stone/benchtop) who visit every kitchen shop in the area. Ask them to mention Paul to owners who complain about
  admin, and pass names. Offer a thank-you per sale (suggest $500, Paul to set).
- Why: reps already have weekly face time with exactly our buyers, and suppliers actively market to these owners
  (Planit and Biesse sponsor ACFA events and run open houses: https://www.acfa.net.au/partner/planit-australia ,
  https://www.biesse.com/au/wood/events/brisbane-open-house). One rep ≈ 20-60 shops. Busy jobs = more product
  ordered, so the rep's interest lines up with ours.
- Cost: 2 x 45 min coffees + travel; $0 until a sale.
- Expected: 1 active rep → 2-4 introductions a quarter. Unproven; the big unknown is whether reps bother.
- Test: 2 coffees by 11 Dec. Keep if either rep passes a name by 22 Jan.

### 3. Founding-customer offer (first 10 builds) — NEW
- What: a named, limited offer: "one of the first 10 businesses": e.g. support plan discount for year 1 or the build
  paid in stages, in return for a case study, a reference call and a referral. Clear end date (e.g. 31 Mar 2027).
- Why: gives emails and visits a reason to act now; standard early-stage B2B tactic. Price gap is real:
  CabiPro (AU, cabinet makers) starts at A$200/month flat (https://www.capterra.com.au/software/1096699/CabiPro);
  our build is a five-figure one-off. Owners will compare. Staged payment lowers the hurdle.
- Cost: margin only; 15 min of Paul's time to set terms.
- Test: add one line to the visit script for 2 weeks after Paul sets the terms. Decide at end of Jan.

### Competitor notes (for positioning)
- CabiPro: AU, cabinet makers/joinery, from A$200/month flat, +A$35 per admin, +A$20 per installer
  (https://www.capterra.com.au/software/1096699/CabiPro).
- Tradify has a page aimed at kitchen businesses (https://www.tradifyhq.com/au/trades/kitchen-business-software);
  general trades job software, monthly per user.
- CabinetTrak (US commercial millwork, US$149-699/month) and ProWorks (UK trades, from £25/user/month) are not
  really in our market.
- Our angle vs all of them: built by a kitchen owner, in the business's own name, around how that shop already
  works; customer and installer apps with their logo. Weak spot: one-off price vs a monthly subscription.
